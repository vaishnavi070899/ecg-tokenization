Why this matters

Bridging cardiac signals and language models

ECG interpretation remains a specialized, bottlenecked skill. Tokenization translates continuous waveforms into discrete symbols that transformer-based LLMs can natively process — unlocking generative AI for clinical cardiology.

Problem
ECG data is continuous, LLMs are discrete
Large language models operate on tokens — finite symbol sequences. Raw ECG waveforms are continuous-time signals. No direct interface exists between them without an explicit discretization step.
Core bottleneck
Approach
VQ-VAE learns a cardiac codebook
A Vector Quantized Variational Autoencoder compresses ECG segments into a finite learned vocabulary. Residual quantization (RVQ) stacks multiple codebook stages to progressively refine representation quality.
This research
Vision
Generative + diagnostic AI for cardiology
Tokenized ECGs can feed into transformer decoders for generation, clinical Q&A, and anomaly detection — analogous to how image tokens power visual LLMs. Downstream classification from token sequences also becomes tractable.

Codebook health states

Four measurable states interpret the joint signal of MSE, Dead Code %, and Perplexity.

Healthy Representation
MSE ↓ low Dead Code % ↓ low Perplexity ↑ high
Codebook is fully utilized, reconstruction is accurate, and codes are evenly distributed across ECG patterns.
Codebook Collapse
MSE ↑ high Dead Code % ↑ high Perplexity ↓ low
Most codes are inactive. Reconstruction fails. The model has collapsed to a small subset of the codebook.
Forced Activation
MSE — variable Dead Code % ↓ low (forced) Perplexity — artificial
Interventions like centroid reset activate dead codes artificially, but the underlying capacity ceiling may still exist.
Over-Compression
MSE ↑ high (floor) Dead Code % ↓ low Perplexity ↑ high
Codes are used but reconstruction quality saturates. The codebook capacity is insufficient relative to ECG signal complexity.
Phase 1 — 16 experiments

Three root causes of codebook collapse

Experiments ruled out naive fixes — entropy regularization, usage penalties, soft assignments — and converged on three structural causes specific to ECG signals.

Cause 1
Training dynamics & objective misalignment
Early training instability causes encoder outputs to cluster, making most codebook entries unreachable. Standard regularization methods redistribute active codes but don't address the structural cause — EMA with k-means++ warm-start corrects this at initialization.
Fixed: EMA + k-means++
Cause 2
Data-driven capacity ceiling (~120 active codes)
PTB-XL Lead I signals contain a bounded number of distinguishable ECG morphologies. At 64-dimensional latent space, each stage saturates at approximately 120 active codes regardless of K, reflecting the intrinsic information content of the signal — not an optimization failure.
Insight: biological limit
Cause 3
Stage hierarchy mismatch in residual quantization
Early RVQ stages receive the full signal; later stages receive only residuals. Uniform K across stages wastes capacity — later stages only need fine-grained codes. Stage-specific K=[256,256,128,64] aligns codebook size to the expected residual entropy at each level.
Fixed: stage-specific K

Why regularization failed

Three intuitive approaches were tested and ruled out structurally.

Method Idea Why it failed
Entropy regularization Penalize uneven code usage in loss Soft optimization target; hard collapse already entrenched by the time penalty fires
Usage penalty Discourage over-used codes Only redistributes active codes — doesn't revive dead ones or increase total active count
Soft assignments Assign input to top-k codes with weights Destroys hard discretization; inter-stage residuals lose meaning in later RVQ stages
What comes next

Phase 2 roadmap

With a stable tokenizer established, the next phase connects ECG tokens to language model architectures and clinical downstream tasks.

01
ECG–Text alignment via contrastive pretraining
Joint embedding space for ECG token sequences and clinical text reports. Validated using UMAP visualization of cross-modal embeddings from PTB-XL diagnostic labels.
02
Generative ECG language modeling
Autoregressive transformer trained on ECG token sequences. Enables conditional generation: given patient history tokens, generate plausible future ECG token sequences.
03
Downstream classification from token sequences
Evaluate whether token-sequence representations achieve competitive diagnostic accuracy on PTB-XL classification benchmarks vs. continuous signal baselines.
04
Extension to multi-lead and multi-modal signals
Generalize the tokenization architecture to all 12 leads and explore cross-signal alignment with EEG and PPG — toward a unified physiological signal language.
